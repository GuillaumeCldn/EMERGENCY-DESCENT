\chapter{Executive summary}
% ===============================================================

This document is the functional specification of the EMERGENCY DESCENT function. It provides “aircraft level” requirements that will be the main input for systems specifications.

The purpose of the EMERGENCY DESCENT function is to have the aircraft descend to a safe altitude in the case of loss of cabin pressurisation.

\chapter{Function Description}
\label{sec:FD}
% ===============================================================

The purpose of the EMERGENCY DESCENT function is to navigate the aircraft from its current altitude to \SI{10000}{ft} or the MSA, following the fastest safe descent profile.

The EMERGENCY DESCENT function provides the following features:

\begin{itemize}
	\item It allows the flight crew to activate the EMERGENCY DESCENT. 
	\item Upon activation of the EMERGENCY DESCENT:
		\subitem The aircraft begins descending to \SI{10000}{ft}, or the published MSA (whichever is greater) at \SI{250}{kts} IAS.
		\subitem It allows the flight crew to select a desired altitude if different the altitude selected by the EMERGENCY DESCENT function.
		\subitem It allows the flight crew to set a desired IAS for the descent. 
	\item Upon arriving to the selected altitude, the aircraft levels off in ALT mode: the EMERGENCY DESCENT function is finished. 
	\item The EMERGENCY DESCENT function can be deactivated at any time by selecting another vertical navigation AP funtion. 
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Operational Scenarios}
\label{sec:OS}
% ===============================================================

In nominal scenario 1, it is an Emergency descent with structural damage, it is assumed that both pilots remain conscious throughout the emergency. 

In nominal scenario 2, it is an Emergency descent without structural damage,it is assumed that both pilots remain conscious throughout the emergency.

\section{Nominal scenario 1}

\textbf{Initial conditions:} The aircraft is flying at a typical A320 cruise altitude, both pilots are in the cockpit, the autopilot and auto-thrust are engaged. An explosive depressurization occurs resulting in
structural damage to the aircraft.

\textbf{STEP 1 :} A visual and an audio alert inform the pilots of the situation. 

\textbf{STEP 2 :} The pilots put on their oxygen mask.

\textbf{STEP 3 :}  The system sets :
\begin{enumerate}
\item the altitude to \SI{10000}{ft}  
\item the speed to \SI{250}{kts} 
\item the heading to Selected Mode
\item  the SPD/MACH knob is set to SPD
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{STEP 4 :} There is an audio announcement of these actions,
and the information is displayed on the FCU. 
The actions will be active (Managed Mode) after a delay and Terrain mode will be displayed on the ND. Meanwhile, pilots can still modify the instructions before they become  active.

\textbf{STEP 5 :} the system alerts the air traffic controller of the situation (Emergency depressurization and Emergency descent) and of the target altitude and speed.

\section{Nominal scenario 2}

\textbf{Initial conditions:} The aircraft is flying at a typical A320 cruise altitude, both pilots are in the cockpit, the autopilot and auto-thrust are engaged. An explosive depressurization occurs without structural damage to the aircraft.

\textbf{STEP 1 :} A visual and audio alert informs the pilots of the situation.

\textbf{STEP 2 :} The pilots put on their oxygen mask.

\textbf{STEP 3 :} The system sets :
\begin{enumerate}
\item the altitude to \SI{10000}{ft}  
\item the speed to \SI{250}{kts} 
\item the heading to Selected Mode
\item the SPD/MACH knob is set to SPD
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{STEP 4 :} There is an audio announcement of these actions,
and the information is displayed on the FCU. 
The actions will be active (Managed Mode) after a delay and Terrain mode will be displayed on the ND. Meanwhile, pilots can still modify the instructions before they become  active.

\textbf{STEP 5 :} pilots set speed to selected mode and increase it to VMO + $\Delta.$.

\textbf{STEP 6 :} the system alerts the air traffic controller of the situation (Emergency depressurization and Emergency descent) and of the target altitude and speed .

\textbf{STEP 7 :} pilots manage the systems and communications with ATC. They deploy
the speed brakes as needed.

\EMERDES{001}{The system shall enable the aircraft to reach an altitude of 10000 ft or MSA, whichever is higher, at maximum vertical speed, without any intervention of the pilot, in less than TBD minutes.}{The system shall operate in any terrain environment, whether the pilots are operational or not.}{}

\EMERDES{002}{The system shall maintain the IAS of the aircraft at 250 kts throughout the whole descent if no pilot override has been provided.}{In case of structural damage, the maximum speed would be 250 kts. Otherwise, the pilots, if conscious, could manually increase it to VMO - delta.}{}

\EMERDES{003}{The system shall set the HDG to Selected Mode and SPD/MACH to SPD in less than TBD seconds after the initial system activation.}{As stated in the EMER DES procedures.}{}

\EMERDES{004}{The system shall display TERR on ND after the delay in less than TBD seconds.}{Terrain should be displayed for situational awareness. This display should happen after the delay, when descent is initiated to avoid overwhelming pilots with information.}{Whether the system should act accordingly to the terrain will be discussed at a later stage.}

\EMERDES{005}{The pilots shall be informed visually and audibly of each action that the system is intending to perform.}{Consequence of the pilot interview. The pilots should be aware of each and every action performed automatically by the system. In case of smoke in the cockpit (explosive decompression), the information should be audible as well.}{}

\EMERDES{006}{The pilots shall be able to cancel any spurious activation of the system with a single action.}{The system should be easily cancellable if spurious.}{}

\EMERDES{007}{The pilots shall be able to modify any parameter set by the system with a single action.}{The system should be easily modifiable by pilots (change of HDG, speed increased to VMO-delta,…).}{}

\EMERDES{008}{The system shall inform the ATC of the EMER DES mode of the aircraft, the target parameters as well as the current vertical speed of the aircraft.}{}{We will see at a later stage if we could use ADS-C or CPDLC for specifying the requirements for ATC communications.}

\EMERDES{009}{The system shall be disengaged as soon as the aircraft is stabilised at 10000 ft or MSA, whichever is higher.}{The system should be disabled when stabilisation is reached, at the lowest “breathable” altitude.}{}

\EMERDES{010}{The system shall be disengaged as soon as any other vertical navigation mode is enabled.}{If the pilots decide to override the system, or when another vertical mode becomes active, the EMER DES system can be disabled.}{}
